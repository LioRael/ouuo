# ouuo design direction

Beautiful interfaces should be playable.

Reading this as a digital toy exhibition for curious people, with the physical presence of a carefully made instrument. ENERGY 3 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 2.

- Paper `#f7f5ef`: a quiet gallery wall, not an app dashboard.
- Ink `#242622`: strongly legible typography and physical controls.
- Vermilion `#b83b25`: the brand's play affordance, used selectively rather than as a background wash.
- plonko uses a quiet tabletop `#efeee9`, a blue molded shell (`#455c97` to `#344b84`), warm-white keys and a coral flip button. The name is lowercase, with a small period on the toy's casing: `plonko.`. It replaces Glass Piano because the approved object is a playful molded-plastic instrument, not a glass one. The owner approved this direction for continued detail refinement. The restrained casing highlight keeps small light lettering legible. The earlier silver shell with a large round speaker and split left/right console was rejected as too similar to ui-chords.
- Celadon key feedback connects touch to sound. The little display greets the player and shows active notes; the speaker perforations and layered key shadows belong to the instrument rather than decorating a web page.
- Use locally served Instrument Sans for the brand and reading text. Its open shapes keep the large wordmark playful without borrowing a gaming typeface. Use system monospace only for key bindings and exact musical values.
- The oversized lowercase wordmark and a single sculptural, playable piano preview carry the homepage. Exhibit labels describe real functionality, not marketing metrics.
- The homepage shows only the completed piano as the main exhibit. Future works are a compact workshop ledger, explicitly labelled in development.
- Piano gradients and contact shadows describe the shell, key bevels and key travel, rather than adding floating web panels. The keyboard spans the full instrument width; a narrow control strip replaces the reference's three oversized mode pads. A centered display, short slotted speaker and coral flip key occupy the top rail. Blue casing and warm-white lettering give the object a playful identity without copying the reference's silhouette. Narrow screens stack these same physical parts.
- A fine inset shell seam follows the casing radius; the dark blue keybed recedes so the warm keys remain the focus. Separate key-front bevels describe thickness in both resting and celadon pressed states. The little display has a restrained corner reflection and pale green musical readout, while rear sliders and switches carry grip details. These are static material cues, never extra controls or idle animation; decorative layers do not intercept touch.
- The ui-chords reference's top volume control is a mostly concealed horizontal wheel, not its large circular joystick. Only a narrow ridged edge protrudes above the casing. Pressing never jumps to an absolute value: horizontal dragging changes volume by one point per two CSS pixels, vertical dragging does nothing, and only the ridges move. The invisible hit area extends over the casing for touch; keyboard arrows and Home/End remain available. Mode, song, sustain and mute controls live on the front so playing needs no settings window.
- The coral other-side button reveals the matching blue rear shell: four corner screws, a warm-white recessed control bay and Music, Practice, Sound and Keys pages. Low-frequency adjustments stay there without adding website chrome. Small rectangular sliding switches suit physical hardware rather than phone settings. Front and back share application state.
- Both sides and all three playing modes share the same shell dimensions and corner profile at a given viewport; neither flipping nor switching modes may resize or reposition the object or its keyboard. All modes use the same inset dark play screen and border: free-play note trails rise within it, while Listen and Challenge show their score cues. The shell reserves the space needed for the challenge. Dimensions respond only to the viewport, never to the selected mode. The rear has one coral flip key in its header, with no duplicate return button, home link or footer row. The header and tabs stay fixed above one recessed content bay, which uses the space recovered from the footer. Wide screens group practice parameters beside the passage editor and audio controls beside light/guide switches; narrow screens stack them and scroll only inside the bay. Compact binding instructions save space without reducing 44px input targets.
- The rear uses the same molded-plastic language as the playable front: four separate cream page keys sit in a dark rail, with a recessed gold selected key. Song choices have shallow cartridge edges and blue windows showing the actual authored notes. Binding fields read as keycaps, parameter values sit in inset numeric windows, and slider ticks describe their travel. Below 360px, binding fields use four columns so both their width and height remain at least 44px. Blue switch wells indicate real on states. Shadows describe millimeters of relief, not floating cards; the casing seam and all grip details are non-interactive. Cream page keys use dark focus rings, while the blue shell keeps its light ring.
- Interaction motion shows key travel, note position, playback progress and a brief 240ms flip into the rear controls. Nothing floats idly. Reduced motion removes the flip transform, decorative transitions and the scrolling note field, retaining static target cues and game timing.
- Desktop is one self-contained instrument; mobile keeps the octave visible, with internal keyboard scrolling below 320px. No hidden hover-only controls. Polyphonic touch, held notes and glissando remain native pointer interactions.
- Pocket Arcade follows the owner's cream/coral/dark-gray/lavender handheld reference, with molded shell bevels, recessed screen and physical button travel. The page contains only the handheld on the same quiet tabletop, without exhibit headings, cards or footer. Instructions, settings and the home link live inside the screen. Landscape places controls beside the display; narrow portrait layouts stack the same parts below it to retain usable touch targets. Pixel art and music are original; shading describes material depth rather than website panels. ENERGY 3 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 2, with no idle floating. The owner's refinement removes casing rotation at every width, not just under reduced motion. A fine concentric seam and shallow bottom edge replace the heavy dark outline; rounded, continuous D-pad geometry and unified press travel describe one molded piece rather than four separate keycaps. Coral and lavender action keys sit in shallow wells; aligned speaker holes and subdued casing print keep the screen dominant. Library rows fill the screen alongside cropped original game artwork, without miniature HUD text in the preview. Reduced motion removes decorative transitions while retaining held-state feedback.

No image generation, stock imagery, copyrighted music or borrowed game sprites are needed. The toy previews are rendered from HTML/CSS; the music is an original short composition.
